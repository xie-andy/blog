## LLMs as a non-deterministic system

- LLMs are a non-deterministic system. If you are not familiar with the concept, it is easier to start off with the definition of a deterministic system. A deterministic system accepts some input, and given that input will produce the same output every time. An example would be a calculator, where you'd hope that typing 1 + 1 gives you 2 every time you enter it.

- Conversely, LLMs are non-deterministic, and you can easily check this by saying hi to your chatbot a couple of times, where you'll see a different response each time. Why? Well, this may require a whole separate blog article on how LLMs work (which will likely be a whole new series at this rate, as **a)** I keep learning about them and **b)** the technology evolves.), but essentially, LLMs predict the next word to generate, and assigns a probability to the each plausible next word.

- For example, suppose I asked you to finish the sentence: The sky is ___. 
- You would probably say "blue" is the next word. However, there are also other plausible words that could fill in the blank, for example "bright", "sunnny", etc. An LLM will predict the next word with percentage probabilities, for example:

    - Blue: 80%
    - Bright: 15%
    - Sunny: 5%

- I hope you can see how it is feasible that the LLM could generate a different sentence even with the same output. From there, models have a variable called "temperature", which will affect how likely the LLM is to choose a less likely option. For example, if the temperature setting of the LLM is high, then it is more likely to pick "bright" over "blue", even if it predicts that "blue" should be much more likely to finish the sentence.

## Making LLMs more deterministic

- This non-deterministic characteristic is useful day to day. When you want your chatbot to come up with something creative, or write in more detail, more complexity, or simpler when you tell it "do it again, but better", then this attribute allows it to generate unique and novel answers.

- This also means that when **you and your entire university class feeds in the exact same assignment specs**, there is some (minute) element of differentiation between your AI slop essays. Now that you know about temperature variables, you can try tweaking your LLM to have a higher temperature variable and therefore produce a vastly different AI slop essay from everyone else in your class.

- However, this characteristic is not as useful when I want it to do something with a defined process. In this case, I want it to execute an exact set of commands: make Google Calendar meeting and then schedule public transport time before. To me, it is not helpful if it decides to schedule a calendar meeting in Apple Calendar instead of Google Calendar, or calculate transport times via walking instead of public transport.

- To try and solve/mitigate this problem, I have begun implementing "skills", which you may have seen in the news as the **latest and greatest** addition to your chatbot of choice.

## Skills

- Skills have recently been implemented in Claude. The idea is pretty similar to my problem: sometimes, you want a chatbot to solve a problem in a structured, fixed manner, where you don't want any ambiguity or creativity.

- The concept of a skill itself is not rocket science. All it is is a file that holds a string of text, instructing an LLM how to solve a problem if it comes up.

- This can greatly improve an LLMs ability to deal with certain problems and situations in a deterministic manner and execute according to a fixed set of instructions. However, there are still a couple of issues:

    - The non-deterministic nature of the LLM still remains. While you are giving it instructions, this simply increases the percentage likelihood that it executes them in a certain way. There is still a world where the model hallucinates/deviates from your instructions due to the aforementioned temperature variable.
    
    - The LLM must recognise when to use the tool in the first place. For example, my LLM is currently trained to recognise me asking for meetings and similar sentiments. However, there is still a chance it doesn't identify the tool in the first place. 
    - For example, if I said "I want to grab lunch with Luka at 1pm at our local cafe", then there is a chance it could invoke the note skill instead of the calendar skill.
